# Iron Age IIA chronology in the southern Levant

## Research question

Did the central Negev fortresses fall within the chronological window of the early Iron IIA (10th c. BCE), as Cohen 1979 argued, or later (9th c. BCE), as the *Low Chronology* of Finkelstein 1999 / 2003 requires?

## Context and motivation

The dating of the central Negev fortresses bears on a larger debate about state formation in the southern Levant: a 10th-century attribution supports the conventional model of a United Monarchy with a substantial Negev presence; a 9th-century attribution reassigns the fortresses to the Omride state-building project. The 14C evidence from Tel Rehov and later sites has reshaped the debate without settling it. This project re-reads the foundational arguments through close engagement with the published reports.

## Methodology

Hermeneutic close reading of the primary monographs (Cohen 1979, Finkelstein 1999 / 2003, Finkelstein & Piasetzky 2003, Mazar 2011) and the *Forschungsstand* literature, with explicit attention to where each side's stratigraphic and ceramic arguments hinge on disputed loci. No new excavation; no quantitative re-analysis (the project is hermeneutic, not quantitative).

## Expected outcomes

A peer-reviewed journal article (~6000 words) tracing the argumentative structure of the chronology debate as it applies to the Negev fortresses specifically, and arguing for either (a) a regional-variation reading that accommodates both positions, or (b) one chronological resolution as more defensible.

## Scope boundaries

Out of scope: re-analysis of 14C data (the project trusts published HPDs), Phoenician chronology (referenced only where it bears on the southern Levant), biblical historiography arguments (referenced as context, not engaged systematically).
