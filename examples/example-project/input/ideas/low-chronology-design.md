## Research Question

Did the central Negev fortresses fall within the chronological window of the early *Iron IIA* (10th c. BCE), as Cohen 1979 argued, or later (9th c. BCE), as the *Low Chronology* of Finkelstein 1999 / 2003 requires?

## Relevance

- **Scholarly:** The Negev fortress dating bears on the broader chronology debate, which in turn carries weight in the historical placement of the so-called "Davidic" / "Solomonic" monumental architecture — at the intersection of archaeology, biblical historiography, and theology. The Negev case is a particularly clear test because the regional dataset is bounded.
- **State of research (*Forschungsstand*):** The debate has been open since Finkelstein 1996/1999. Cohen's original High Chronology reading of the central Negev (1979) has been challenged but not systematically re-examined in light of subsequent 14C work and revised regional surveys.
- **Own contribution:** A close hermeneutic reading of the argumentative structure of each side, tracing where the positions hinge on specific disputed loci (e.g. Stratum IVA at Megiddo, Tel Rehov phasing, Negev fortress ceramic typology). The aim is not new data but a clearer map of *what is actually being argued about*.

## Method

Hermeneutic close reading, in three passes:

1. **Forschungsstand** — systematic survey of the chronology-debate literature 1979–2025, identifying the three or four argumentative pivots.
2. **Source-by-source close reading** of the foundational positions (Cohen 1979; Finkelstein 1999, 2003; Finkelstein & Piasetzky 2003; Mazar 2005, 2011; Regev et al. 2020), each ingested under a focus tailored to its contribution to the Negev-fortress sub-question.
3. **Synthesis** of the argument structure: where each side rests on which evidence, where the disagreements are about data, where about interpretation, where about framework choice.

No new excavation; no quantitative re-analysis of published 14C data (this project trusts the published HPDs and engages with the methodology rather than re-running it).

## Data sources

- Cohen 1979 — original High Chronology argument for the central Negev
- Finkelstein 1999 — Low Chronology methodology paper
- Finkelstein 2003 — Low Chronology applied to Megiddo, Hazor, Gezer, and (briefly) the Negev
- Finkelstein & Piasetzky 2003 — 14C reconciliation at Tel Rehov
- Mazar 2011 — Modified Conventional Chronology counter-position
- Regev et al. 2020 — current Tel Rehov 14C dataset and re-modelling
- *Forschungsstand* literature: ~15 further A/B-rated sources from a literature-review pass

## Output target

**Journal article** (~6,000 words), aimed at *Tel Aviv* or *Near Eastern Archaeology*.

## Expected outcomes

This is hermeneutic, not quantitative, so there is no frozen hypothesis. Plausible interpretive outcomes the close reading might support:

- A *regional-variation reading* — the Negev fortresses may sit in a different chronological window than the northern monumental architecture, allowing both Cohen and Finkelstein to be partially right.
- A *one-resolution reading* — one chronological position turns out more defensible for the Negev specifically.
- A *Forschungsstand reading* — the deepest disagreement is at the level of *what counts as evidence*, not at the level of the dates themselves; the project's contribution would then be to make this explicit.

The article will commit to whichever reading the close reading best supports. This is a hermeneutic-circle commitment, not a pre-registered prediction.

## Risks

- **Source-availability:** Cohen 1979 is in print but not OA — university library access required.
- **Author bias:** the author has theological training, which colours the reading of the historiographic context. Mandatory adversarial peer review by an archaeologist (via `requesting-peer-review`).
- **Scope creep:** the chronology debate touches biblical historiography; the project must hold the line at "what does this say about Negev fortress dating", not "what does this say about David and Solomon".

## Feasibility

- **Timeframe:** ~3 months from project start to first complete draft. Peer review and revision: additional 4–6 weeks.
- **Tools available:** plugin, Quarto, Zotero with institutional licence, IxTheo + Propylaeum + Zenon-DAI for literature, optional `dao-paper-search-mcp` for verified citations.
- **Literature access:** University library + DAI Zenon + Propylaeum.

## Scope boundaries

- **NOT addressed:** re-analysis of 14C raw data (trusts published HPDs)
- **NOT addressed:** Phoenician chronology (referenced only where it bears on the southern Levant)
- **NOT addressed:** biblical historiography arguments (referenced as context, not engaged systematically)
- **NOT addressed:** *Iron Age I* / pre-Iron-IIA strata (separate debate)

## Ethics / open science

- All quotations from sources with page numbers.
- Wiki and manuscript drafts versioned in git.
