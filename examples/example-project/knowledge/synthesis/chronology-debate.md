# Chronology Debate, Iron Age IIA — Negev Fortress Lens

## Research question

How does the *Low Chronology* / *Modified Conventional Chronology* debate apply specifically to the dating of the central Negev fortresses (Cohen 1979)? The general debate is well-traced in the literature; this synthesis filters it through the Negev case as a regional test.

## Argument structure of the debate

The debate operates on four levels. Each side hinges on a different one — disagreements at deeper levels look like data disputes when they are actually framework disputes.

### Level 1 — Data selection

*Which measurements count as phase-diagnostic?* Finkelstein & Piasetzky (2003) argue for restricting the corpus to short-lived samples (charred olive pits) from clear stratigraphic contexts — see [[finkelstein-piasetzky-2003]] §"Focus: 14C reconciliation". Mazar's counter-position ([[mazar-2011]], stub) extends the corpus to longer-lived samples and contests several of Finkelstein's stratigraphic phase assignments. Regev et al. ([[regev-et-al-2020]], stub) update the Tel Rehov dataset with newer measurements and a tighter calibration — narrowing but not closing the gap between the two readings.

### Level 2 — Calibration

The Low Chronology argument was originally calibrated with IntCal98 ([[finkelstein-piasetzky-2003]], p. 295). Subsequent recalibration under IntCal13 and IntCal20 tightens HPDs by ~10 years but does not change the direction of inference. This is not the seat of the disagreement.

### Level 3 — Phase modelling

Finkelstein & Piasetzky use uniform priors on phase boundaries; Mazar argues for informative priors derived from stratigraphic seriation. This is a methodological choice with non-trivial consequences — it shapes whether the posterior centres on the Low or the Modified Conventional reading.

### Level 4 — Framework choice (what counts as evidence)

The deepest disagreement is not about 14C dates but about *what role 14C plays*. For Finkelstein, 14C is the arbiter when stratigraphy is contested. For Mazar, stratigraphy and ceramic typology remain primary and 14C is supplementary. This is where the chronology debate is structurally hardest to resolve — the disputants are not measuring different things; they are arguing about which evidence has priority.

## The Negev fortress sub-question

The central Negev fortresses (Cohen 1979, *ingest pending*) sit at a particular point in this structure:

- The 14C dataset for the Negev specifically is thin compared with Tel Rehov or Megiddo — so the debate at Levels 1–3 is partly transferred from the northern sites.
- The ceramic typology of the central Negev is locally distinctive, raising the question whether the Iron IIA pottery from there should be aligned with the Beersheva Valley typology or treated as a separate horizon. This is a Level-4-flavoured question (which comparative framework is primary).
- Cohen's original argument was framed before the chronology debate; he could not have anticipated either the Low Chronology or Mazar's counter-position. His evidence is still on the table, but the *framework* in which to read it has shifted twice since 1979.

## Evidence currently in the wiki

- [[finkelstein-piasetzky-2003]] §"Focus: 14C reconciliation" — claim: the *Iron IIA* onset is 925 ± 20 BCE (95% HPD), compatible with Low Chronology, incompatible with a 10th-c. monumental-architecture reading (pp. 293–294). Boundary noted: does not include central Negev or Beersheva Valley data; uses IntCal98.
- [[mazar-2011]] — stub. Claim: Modified Conventional Chronology accommodates most of the 14C evidence by adjusting phase priors rather than shifting absolute dates; the gap with Finkelstein is much narrower than the debate's tone suggests.
- [[regev-et-al-2020]] — stub. Claim: the current Tel Rehov dataset under IntCal20 still supports a Low-Chronology direction but with tighter HPDs that reduce — without eliminating — the disagreement with Mazar.

## Open questions

- How much does the central Negev case actually depend on the northern sites? A focused close reading of Cohen 1979 (next ingest) should answer this.
- Is there a *regional-variation reading* under which Cohen and Finkelstein are both partly right — Cohen's 10th-c. horizon for the central Negev plus Finkelstein's 9th-c. shift for the northern monumental architecture?
- At which level (1–4) does the Negev sub-debate primarily live? If it's Level 4, the synthesis chapter should make that explicit as the structural finding.

## Implications (provisional)

The synthesis tentatively favours a Forschungsstand reading: the deepest Negev-specific disagreement is at Level 4 (what counts as primary evidence for a regional horizon), not at the level of the 14C measurements themselves. Confirming this requires the Cohen 1979 ingest and at least one Mazar/Cohen-comparison synthesis pass. Status: `review` until then.
