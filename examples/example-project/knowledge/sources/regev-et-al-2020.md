## Bibliographic Reference

Regev, Johanna; Finkelstein, Israel; Adams, Matthew J. & Boaretto, Elisabetta (2020): Radiocarbon Chronology of Iron Age Tel Rehov under IntCal20. In: *Ägypten und Levante* 30, pp. 421–446. DOI: [10.1553/AEundL30s421](https://doi.org/10.1553/AEundL30s421).

BibTeX key: `@regev-et-al-2020` (see `output/bibtex/references.bib`).

## Focus: the current Tel Rehov 14C dataset and re-modelling — 2026-05-28

### Claims relevant to this focus

1. The expanded [[tel-rehov]] 14C dataset (n = 73 short-lived samples from phases V–IV) under IntCal20 places the *Iron I/IIA* transition at 925 ± 12 BCE (68% HPD), tightening the original 2003 estimate without shifting its central tendency (pp. 428–432).
2. Bayesian phase modelling with informative priors derived from stratigraphic seriation (i.e. the Mazar approach) yields a marginally later transition at ~915 BCE — within the HPD of the uniform-prior model (pp. 433–435).
3. The 14C evidence is therefore *compatible with both* the *Low Chronology* and the *Modified Conventional Chronology*; the remaining disagreement at Tel Rehov is at the level of phase assignment, not absolute dates (pp. 436–438).
4. Inter-laboratory differences (Groningen, Oxford, Mannheim) remain at ~8 years systematic offset — consistent with the original 2003 estimate of ~10 years (p. 440).

### Direct quotes (supporting the above)

> "Under IntCal20, the Iron I/IIA transition at Tel Rehov is constrained to 925 ± 12 BCE (68% HPD), narrowing the 2003 estimate by roughly 8 years without shifting its central tendency." (p. 432)

> "The data themselves do not adjudicate between the Low and the Modified Conventional Chronology; the choice between them is now a methodological one about phase-modelling priors." (p. 438)

### Boundary: what this source does NOT address (within this focus)

The paper concentrates on [[tel-rehov]] specifically; it does not include data from the central Negev fortresses, the Beersheva Valley, or the southern desert sites. It also does not engage the historiographic question of how the chronology debate has narrowed over time — for that, see [[mazar-2011]]. The OxCal input files are published as supplementary on the journal's site but are not reproduced in the article body.

## Other content in this source

A long methodological appendix (pp. 440–446) on Bayesian phase-modelling with informative priors versus uniform priors, including a worked example. This material would be relevant to a re-ingest focused on phase-modelling methodology specifically (not currently in scope of this project's focus).
