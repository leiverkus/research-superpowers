# Finkelstein 2003 — Low Chronology Revisited {#sec-source-finkelstein-2003}

## Bibliographic Details

Finkelstein, I. (2003). The Low Chronology and the Problem of the Archaeology of Iron Age Palestine. *Tel Aviv* 30(2), 149–174. DOI: [10.1179/tav.2003.2003.2.149](https://doi.org/10.1179/tav.2003.2003.2.149).

BibTeX key: `@finkelstein-2003-low-chronology`.

## Focus: the Low Chronology argument for Iron IIA in the southern Levant — 2026-04-17

### Claims relevant to this focus

1. The conventional chronology of Iron Age I–IIA in the southern Levant is set roughly 50–75 years too high (pp. 149–151).
2. The monumental architecture traditionally attributed to Solomon at [[entity-tel-megiddo]], Hazor, and Gezer originates from the Omride dynasty (9th c. BCE), not the 10th (pp. 158–163).
3. Stratigraphic re-analysis of Stratum IVA at [[entity-tel-megiddo]] supports placing the six-chambered gate in the early 9th c. (p. 162).

### Direct quotes (supporting the above)

> "The conventional chronology of the Iron Age I–IIA strata in the Levant should be lowered by some 50–75 years, with the Iron IIA spanning the late 10th to mid-9th century BCE." (p. 151)

> "The monumental buildings at Megiddo Stratum IVA, Hazor X, and Gezer VIII belong to the Omride era, not the Solomonic." (p. 162)

### Boundary: what this source does NOT address (within this focus)

The article concentrates on the southern Levant; northern sites in the Beqaa Valley and Phoenician chronology are referenced only in passing. The 14C dating evidence is summarised rather than re-analysed — for that, see Finkelstein & Piasetzky 2003 in the same volume.

## Focus: implications for Negev fortress dating — 2026-05-14

### Claims relevant to this focus

1. The chronology revision implies that the central Negev fortresses, traditionally dated to the 10th c. (Cohen 1979), should be re-attributed to the 9th c. (pp. 169–172).
2. Finkelstein argues this aligns the Negev fortress system with Omride state-building rather than United-Monarchy expansion (p. 171).

### Direct quotes (supporting the above)

> "If the Low Chronology holds, the central Negev fortresses are an Omride frontier system, not a Solomonic one." (p. 171)

### Boundary: what this source does NOT address (within this focus)

The 2003 article does not re-examine the Negev fortress ceramic typology directly — it argues from the broader chronology framework. For the ceramic argument, see Singer-Avitz 2006 (not yet ingested).

## Other content in this source

The article also discusses Egyptian synchronism arguments (Shoshenq I campaign, pp. 153–157) and the implications for biblical historiography (pp. 172–174).
